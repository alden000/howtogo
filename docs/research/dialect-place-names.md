# Old dialect & colloquial place names — research draft

> Status: **DRAFT for owner review. Not wired into the app.** Machine-readable copy: [dialect-place-names.json](dialect-place-names.json).
> Prepared by PM (HowToGo team), 2026-11-20.

## Why
Elderly users say or type the names they grew up with — 坡底, 牛车水, 四排坡, 乌桥头 — not OneMap's English names. This list proposes an alias for each one so the app can route to a current place that OneMap knows.

## Method
1. **Names.** Started from the brief's list. Added names from the Singaporean Mandarin Database (新加坡华语资料库, Promote Mandarin Council — the most authoritative online source for these terms), a 2011 联合早报 piece on old street nicknames, NHB/Roots heritage-trail material and NLB Infopedia. Remember Singapore and Wikipedia were used **only as leads**; entries sourced only from them are marked medium or low.
2. **Targets.** For each name I picked a current place OneMap knows (MRT station, market, temple, hospital, road). Where an elder would usually mean a specific building (e.g. 四马路 → the Kwan Im temple), that building comes first.
3. **Coordinates** come from the app's own search, `/api/search?step=1` (OneMap, no AI step), on 2026-11-20. That was 76 requests, at least 1.6 s apart. Each target records OneMap's exact name and postal code. Roads, bridges and MRT exits have no postal code.
4. **Confidence.** **high**: an authoritative source confirms what the name refers to, and the target is obvious. **medium**: the referent is sourced but the target or extent is a judgement call, or there are only secondary sources. **low**: unconfirmed — every such entry is marked **?** with a question to ask an elder.

**Totals: 77 names — 43 high, 22 medium, 12 low.**

## How to review
- Tick or fix each row. Pay most attention to the **Notes** column (ambiguity) and to every row marked **?**.
- Where several targets are listed, the app should offer a "did you mean" choice (biggest font, first option = most likely). Do not auto-route these.
- Rows sourced only as "Official Chinese place name" are standard Chinese names (e.g. 后港, 加冷). They are listed so the Chinese spelling resolves; they need no dialect check.
- Some names are inauspicious or offensive (死人街, 吉宁, 疯人院). Accept them as input but never show them back to the user.
- Romanisations are typical spellings elders and older signs use. Expect more variants in real use.

## Known gaps
- **Not covered:** Teochew- and Hainanese-specific names (e.g. coffee-shop-street names), most estate-level names (each town has its own), and bus-terminal nicknames. Elders mostly use official terminal names. I found no authoritative list of terminal nicknames.
- Single-character lookalikes and typos (坡低, 牙笼) are listed as variants. Simplified/traditional pairs (後港/后港) should be handled by normalisation, not the alias table.

## Owner questions (uncertain — please check with an elder)

The two names the owner asked about first:
- **黑桥头 / 乌桥头 (Orh Kio Tau).** The Singaporean Mandarin Database says it is the Havelock Road / Delta Road junction, named after a black iron bridge that once stood on Delta Road. Remember Singapore puts it at Beo Crescent, about 300 m away. Both point to the same neighbourhood. Proposed target: Beo Crescent Market, with Havelock MRT (TEL) as second choice. **Watch out:** 乌桥 / 黑桥 *without* 头 means Balestier Road (an old black wooden bridge over the Whampoa River). The app should ask "合乐路/河水山那边，还是马里士他？"
- **结霜桥 (Kiat Sng Kio).** Confidently Sungei Road, around Sim Lim Tower (森林大厦). The name comes from the bridge over Rochor Canal that led to an ice factory, which closed in 1985. The bridge, the factory and the Sungei Road flea market (closed 2017) are all gone. Proposed target: Sim Lim Tower, with Jalan Besar MRT as second choice. One secondary source puts it on the north bank of Rochor — confirm with an elder.
